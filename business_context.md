## PART 1: NEGEV LABS

### Overview
Negev Labs is a venture builder focused on brain health, neuropsychiatry, and longevity-linked indications. The company operates as a holding company (Negev Labs Holdings LLC) that acquires or develops intellectual property, validates drug candidates, forms spin-out companies, advances assets to clinical proof-of-concept (PoC), and seeks exit via pharma M&A, licensing, or IPO.

**Primary current value driver:** Ariadne Bio (AB-300 program).

**Strategic thesis:** Bridge the gap between academic discovery and traditional VC/pharma by validating assets to clinical PoC before large institutional capital enters.

### Mission and Strategic Lens
**Mission:** Extend healthspan (quality of life) alongside lifespan.

Core focus areas: Parkinson's disease apathy, late-life depression, neuropsychiatric disorders, motivation and vitality restoration in aging, brain longevity.

Internal framing often uses terms such as: vitality gap, restoring drive, healthspan drug development. Negev views mental and neurological health as central pillars of longevity.

### Corporate Structure

**NL Founders & Management LLC** — Owners: Ken Belotsky, Shlomi Raz, Kostia Adamsky. Founders' governance vehicle. Economic right: 25% carry of residual profits at HoldCo level. Equity type: Class A.

**Negev Labs Holdings LLC (HoldCo)** — Owns IP, forms spin-outs, controls subsidiaries, allocates capital across portfolio. Equity classes: Class A (Founders — governance + 25% carry), Class B (External investors — preferred return + board rights), Class C (Founders, employees, early stakeholders).

**HoldCo Economic Waterfall:** Class B investors receive 100% return of invested capital (1x preference). Class A (Founders) receive 25% of remaining profits (carry). Remaining 75% distributed pro rata among Class B and Class C.

**Spin-Out Model:** Negev forms portfolio companies around validated assets. HoldCo initially retains majority ownership. Spin-outs have separate capitalization tables. HoldCo investors may have co-investment rights. Strategic control maintained until dilution events. Example: Ariadne Bio.

### Investment Thesis
Negev Labs is positioned as a venture studio for neuropsychiatric and longevity-linked drugs, a capital-efficient bridge between academia and pharma, a portfolio builder reducing single-asset binary risk, and a founder-controlled platform with structured investor protections.

Exit targets: Pharma M&A, licensing deals, IPO at spin-out level.

### Portfolio Strategy
Core principles: Focus on biologically validated mechanisms, small capital-efficient early validation, predefined go/no-go criteria, kill programs early if data fails, concentrate capital post-validation, diversify risk across multiple programs.

Common internal metrics: rNPV (risk-adjusted net present value), probability of technical success (PoTS), capital efficiency, time to PoC, milestone-based financing.

### Risk Framework
Key risks: Clinical failure, capital market volatility, execution risk, regulatory delays, concentration risk (current reliance on Ariadne Bio). Mitigation: Portfolio diversification, spin-out optionality, capital-efficient development, structured waterfall alignment.

## PART 2: ARIADNE BIO

### Company Overview
Ariadne Bio is a clinical-stage biotechnology company developing AB-300, a non-hallucinogenic 5-HT2A/2C agonist, for the treatment of motivational deficits in neurodegenerative and neuropsychiatric disorders.

Initial development focus: **Parkinson's Disease (PD) Apathy**

Longer-term expansion: Late-Life Depression (LLD), other dopamine-related motivational disorders, broader non-motor symptom domains in Parkinson's Disease.

Ariadne Bio originated from Negev Labs' venture creation platform and builds on rediscovered historical clinical data combined with modern pharmacology and translational neuroscience.

### Core Asset: AB-300

AB-300 is a deuterated analog of (R)-dimoxamine, a 5-HT2A/2C partial agonist, non-hallucinogenic, designed for chronic repeat dosing in vulnerable populations (e.g., elderly PD patients).

**What Makes It Different:**
- Unlike classical psychedelics: no hallucinations, no supervised sessions, scalable outpatient pharmaceutical
- Unlike dopaminergic drugs: does not directly stimulate dopamine receptors, modulates motivational circuitry indirectly via serotonin-dopamine interaction
- Unlike SSRIs: SSRIs may worsen apathy; AB-300 targets motivational circuitry directly
- Unlike atypical antipsychotics: avoids sedation-based symptom suppression, targets motivational restoration

### Scientific Hypothesis

In Parkinson's Disease: progressive dopaminergic degeneration, disruption of fronto-striatal motivational circuits, high prevalence of apathy (~40–70%), no FDA-approved therapies specifically for PD-related apathy.

5-HT2A/2C modulation may influence dopaminergic tone in motivational circuits, improve effort-based behavior under dopamine-depleted conditions, and restore goal-directed behavior independent of primary motor improvement.

Preclinical TBZ models show: dopamine depletion → low-effort bias; AB-300 → improvement in effort-based responding; SSRI comparator → worsening performance.

**Key prediction:** If 5-HT2A modulation meaningfully impacts motivation under dopamine deficit, this should produce directional improvement on validated apathy scales in PD patients.

### Historical Clinical Context

The parent compound (R)-dimoxamine was developed by Bristol-Myers in the 1970s, dosed in hundreds of patients, observed to be non-hallucinogenic, and showed signal in Parkinson's patients (open-label data). Program discontinued for strategic, not safety, reasons.

AB-300 is designed to preserve pharmacology, improve PK and CNS exposure, meet modern GLP/ICH-GCP/FDA expectations, and provide composition-of-matter IP protection into the 2040s.

### Development Strategy

**Why PD Apathy First:** Biologically coherent dopamine-decline model, validated apathy scales (LARS, SAS, NPI-Caregiver), measurable functional readouts, clear unmet need, emerging regulatory precedent in non-motor symptoms.

**Phase Ib Objectives:** Not designed to prove efficacy. Designed to determine: directional signal vs placebo on validated apathy scales, consistency across functional readouts, tolerable dose and exposure in PD patients, endpoint acceptability to FDA, viable regulatory path.

**Plan A (Preferred):** Primary endpoint: validated apathy scale. Label target: improvement in PD-related apathy.
**Plan B (Fallback):** Primary endpoint: accepted functional measure. Apathy retained as key secondary.

### Value Inflection

Major inflection point: Positive Phase Ib signal in PD apathy. Unlocks: Phase II readiness, regulatory pathway clarity, expansion into additional PD domains, potential extension into late-life depression, strategic partnering discussions.

**Financing Context:** Bridge round funds Phase Ib execution, IND-enabling work, pre-IND FDA engagement, Phase II design preparation. Capital efficiency principle: the Phase Ib must produce a clear go/no-go decision.

## PART 3: MEETING INTELLIGENCE GUIDANCE

### Meeting Types & What Matters

| Meeting Type | Key Signals to Capture | Typical Action Items |
|---|---|---|
| Investor pitch | Interest level, objections, timeline to decision, DD requests | Send deck, share data room, schedule follow-up, intro to partners |
| Pharma BD call | Therapeutic area fit, internal champion, budget/timeline | Send capabilities deck, schedule scientific deep-dive, provide data package |
| Advisory board | Strategic recommendations, competitive insights | Implement recommendations, schedule next session |
| Portfolio update | Milestones hit/missed, blockers, budget status | Resolve blockers, update investors, adjust timelines |
| VC partner meeting | Fund thesis alignment, co-invest interest | Share deal memo, intro to portfolio company CEO |
| KOL engagement | Scientific validation, trial design input | Incorporate feedback, send updated protocol, add to SAB pipeline |

### Extraction Priorities
1. **Commitments made** — who promised to do what, by when
2. **Interest signals** — language indicating enthusiasm, hesitation, or objections
3. **Relationship development** — new intros requested, warm handoffs, referrals
4. **Deal-relevant data** — timeline mentions, budget discussions, decision criteria
5. **Competitive intelligence** — mentions of other companies, competing approaches
6. **Regulatory/clinical insights** — FDA feedback, endpoint discussions, trial design input
7. **Next steps** — explicitly stated next actions and implicit ones (e.g., "let me check with my team" = follow up in 1 week)

### Transcript Interpretation Guidance
- Distinguish between HoldCo-level and spin-out-level decisions
- Identify whether the topic concerns governance, capital allocation, clinical progress, or exit strategy
- Consider waterfall implications when liquidity or exits are discussed
- Evaluate program discussions through the lens of capital efficiency and probability-adjusted outcomes
- Recognize longevity and healthspan positioning as part of the broader strategic narrative
- Are discussions focused on motor vs non-motor positioning?
- Is apathy framed as primary claim or secondary differentiation?
- Is regulatory strategy (Plan A vs Plan B) clearly articulated?
- Are mechanistic arguments grounded in dopamine-serotonin circuit logic?
- Is historical parent-compound data being used appropriately as context—not proof?
- Is the Phase Ib framed as signal-seeking rather than confirmatory?

### Common Internal Terminology
PoC — Clinical proof-of-concept | Spin-out — New company formed around an asset | Carry — Founder participation after capital return | Preferred — Class B 1x capital protection | Healthspan — Quality-adjusted lifespan | Vitality — Functional motivation and emotional drive | Bridge round — Financing prior to value inflection | Milestone re-rating — Valuation step tied to clinical progress | rNPV — Risk-adjusted net present value | PoTS — Probability of technical success
